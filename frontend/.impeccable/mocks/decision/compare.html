The Cook's Tide Table vs Forecast Desk

Same cook in both: a 5.4 kg brisket flat on a kamado, target 195°F. The top row shows 12:30 PM, in the stall. The bottom row shows 6:38 PM, the pull moment. These are HTML sketches, not final designs: they show each direction's structure, palette, type and signature chart. Example data only.

In the stall, 12:30 PM

The hard moment for a prediction: the temperature isn't moving, so the app has to say how long the stall will last and how sure it is.

The pull moment, 6:38 PM

The most important state in the product. It has to be unmistakable at a glance from across the yard, and the main action has to be the obvious button.

The Cook's Tide Table

  • Distinctive: a yellow band and condensed figures don't look like any thermometer app.
  • Answers "when?" first: the table of predicted times is the core of the screen.
  • The core temp is the biggest thing on screen, readable from a distance.
  • Risk: the almanac's small print can creep back in.

Forecast Desk

  • Shows uncertainty most honestly: the cone visibly widens into the future.
  • Advisory and warning banners are a familiar, instantly read alert language.
  • Calmer and more conventional: closer to a well-made weather or chart app.
  • Risk: familiar; less of a recognizable identity.
